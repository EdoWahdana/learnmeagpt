# learn me a gpt

**[learnmeagpt.com](https://learnmeagpt.com)**

A free, open guide to how GPT-style language models work, written so that anyone can follow it.

## Purpose

Learning how GPT actually works is weirdly hard. Most explanations land in one of two places. Some stay so high-level they don't really explain anything: "it predicts the next word," and that's it. Others jump straight into papers and equations that assume you already know the field. There isn't much out there for someone who just wants to understand what's going on under the hood, one step at a time.

This site is for everyone stuck between those two. It follows your message after you press send, one small step at a time. Your words get cut into little pieces. The pieces turn into numbers. The numbers get mixed together, and the model picks the next small piece of text. Then it does the whole thing again, and again, until the reply is done.


## Goal

A reader with no technical background should be able to start at the first page and, by the end, understand:

- what a GPT is, and what it is not
- why it sometimes sounds confident and is still wrong
- what happens to a message after it is sent
- how a real, public model (GPT-2 small) computes its answer, step by step
- how newer open models build on the same design

Readers who want more depth can continue from the beginner pages into the technical ones, then into interactive tools that run the same calculations in the browser.

## How the site explains things

- **Plain language first.** Every term is defined the first time it appears. The short answer comes first, and the precise version follows.
- **One concrete example at a time.** Ideas are shown with small, real numbers before any symbols or code.
- **Accurate, not hyped.** No invented statistics, no made-up model internals, and no fear or marketing language. When an example uses illustrative numbers, the page says so.
- **Objective.** The pages describe how the system works. They do not tell personal stories or take sides on products.
- **Open models only.** A real model's internals are shown only when its weights are public. Closed chat products are described, never inspected.

## Inspiration

This project is heavily inspired by [learnmeabitcoin.com](https://learnmeabitcoin.com/), which explains Bitcoin from the ground up more clearly than almost anything else written about it. learn me a gpt aims to do the same for language models.

## Contributing

The long-term aim is for this to become a shared resource, improved by anyone who cares about clear education on AI. Contributions are welcome through pull requests.

Useful contributions include:

- **Clearer explanations.** Rewording a confusing paragraph, adding a better everyday comparison, or splitting a dense idea into steps.
- **Corrections.** Fixing a fact, a number, or a misleading simplification. Please include a source or show how the number was computed.
- **New pages.** Many planned topics are not written yet. The [map](https://learnmeagpt.com/map) lists them.
- **Diagrams and interactive tools.** Visuals that make an idea easier to see, or small calculators that let readers try it themselves.
- **Translations and accessibility improvements.** Anything that lets more people read and use the site.
- **Design and usability.** Anything that makes the site easier or more pleasant to read.

Before opening a pull request, please read a few existing pages and match their voice: short sentences, defined terms, real examples, and no first person. If you are unsure whether an idea fits, open an issue first to discuss it.

## Feedback

Found something unclear or wrong? Open an issue. Pointing out a confusing sentence is a contribution too.
